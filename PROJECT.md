# Project plan

This file outlines the project, mechanically, in terms of what I want to get
done when.

## General theses

- Notions of play: A disambiguation -- what is meant by "play?" How can we move it into a workable definition for our purposes?
  - Mechanical play, transformative play -- we focus on transformative play
  - Try to avoid metaphysics here. The goal is _epistemology,_ loosely.
- Play as the method of aesthetic inquiry
  - What is the aesthetic?
- Play at the heart of conceptualization
  - The space 'between words', unconscious play, the work which takes place between symbolisms.
  - Conceptualization as the production of concepts, of values, etc. See D&G _What is Philosophy?_
- Play and Conscription
  - Hans' violent production and creative production.
- Design and Metagaming
  - The role of the artist-philosopher as designer, in the sense of 'game design' -- to create _worlds_ in which meanings become salient.

The general thesis is that play is a powerful and underutilized mechanism for
analyzing our concepts and conceptual apparatuses. Play is a _technique,_ one
which is vital for any field which wants to re-orient us. Reorientation is the
role of aesthetic work: great artwork does not answer questions but raises them,
problematizes.

## Timeline

1. Disambiguate notions of play.
2. Define the _role_ of play in this system. We've noted an emphasis on the
   _function_ of play, rather than its defining characteristics. Play is itself a
   fuzzy concept, perhaps defined as "free movement within a more rigid structure."
   Laying out what that means is precarious -- do we want to include the 'play' of
   a loose mechanical fitting? Do we want to focus purely on agentic play, that of
   humans? Can something broader be said about consciousness and intelligence in
   this manner? (consider the phrase 'entropy is intelligence' -> compression =
   prediction, thus entropy = intelligence in a data theoretical sense -- can
   something similar be said about play, where the stricter the structures the more
   intelligent, the more 'compact', the more predictive?)
3. Define the aesthetic viz Noë, relate it to Hans et al by defending the notion
   that play is the _method_ of aesthetic inquiry. Defense against D&G's three
   distinct categories (philosopher, artist, scientist) as strictly distinct.
   - The aesthetic as bringing us to aporia (skeptic suspension of judgement) --
     method is _elenchus_ or _aporetic inquiry,_ at least it is _a_ method of the
     aesthetic. This is what I termed "problematizing."
   - The movement between finite and infinite is important here: the movement is
     between structured play and free play. Philosophy aims to take something
     from free play and bring it back to structured play. Aesthetics aims to
     destructure our structured play. Science aims to structure our free play.
     Each has their value. Each can be used for ill. Too much of one calls for
     another. Philosophy as living playfully, in between the scientific and the
     artistic? -- D&G say this is impossible, that the artist and the
     philosopher are _distinct personae,_ but I'm inclined to disagree.
4. Explore the psychoanalytic background underlying D&G: Lacan, Freud, etc.:
   trauma as a rupture in the symbolic order -- play as the method by which
   re-symbolization takes place (Derrida)
   - this one reads as something of an appendix or a natural application of the
     overall project, maybe won't fit in the final draft. seems REALLY important
     though -- really begins to talk about the unconscious and its relation to
     epistemology (concept-production)
5. The role of design: explain the role of the philosopher-artist as relating us
   to the infinite and reorienting us.

For each of these subprojects we need to write an essay. The final book will be
a collection of these essays, edited to flow.

## Kinds of outline

### I. Dialectical reconstruction

Use this when the original position contains something you want to preserve.

1.  State a compelling but limited concept.
2.  Expose its internal tension.
3.  Reconstruct it in a form that preserves its insight while overcoming the limitation.
4.  Show how the reconstructed concept reorganizes the problem.

### II. Immanent critique and proposal

Use this when an existing framework fails to explain something important.

1.  Reconstruct the framework charitably.
2.  Show where it fails by its own standards.
3.  Identify what that failure requires of a better account.
4.  Propose the new account.
5.  Explore its consequences and possible limitations.

## Some notes on argumentative structure:

### 1. Elenchus, aporetic inquiry

Method of bringing us into skeptical suspension of belief -- the method of the
aesthetic, to an extent: to bring us _into_ the infinite and leave us there, in
a productive wondering.

### 2. Platonic ascent

> examples -> comparisons -> abstraction -> higher principle -> grounding in experience

This is a movement from general to abstract. It is a matter of class-making.
Proof by induction.

### 3. _Quaestio_

Scholastic. Strict form:

1. State a precise question.
2. Present the strongest objections to the anticipated answer.
3. Introduce an authoritative or contrary consideration: sed contra.
4. Give the central response: respondeo.
5. Reply individually to each objection.

Closely resembles proof-by-contradiction.

### 4. Classical rhetorical argumentation

1. Exordium: establish the problem and your relation to the audience.
2. Narratio: narrate the relevant situation or history.
3. Partitio: state what the argument will establish.
4. Confirmatio: present the positive case.
5. Refutatio: answer opposing views.
6. Peroratio: gather the argument and intensify its stakes.

This is pretty much the classic argumentative / scholarly essay form.

### 5. Montaignian _essai_

Great for fuzzy concepts. Seems to be the argumentative structure of _The Entanglement,_ and much of continental philosophy, e.g. Byung-Chul Han.

> occasion -> exploration -> digression -> self-correction -> provisional insight

The idea is that the concept exists _throughout_ the exploration; " Instead of
suppressing uncertainty, it makes the movement of judgment visible. The writer
tests a concept against memories, quotations, cases, moods, and contradictions.
Its unity comes from a recurring preoccupation rather than a linear proof."

### 6. Meditation and exercise

Cartesian meditation.

> suspend commitments -> undertake exercise -> encounter resistance -> reconcile into a new standpoint

### 7. Phenomenological description

The epoché and eidetic variation.

" A particularly useful technique is eidetic variation: imaginatively change
features of an example to discover which are essential. Remove pleasure,
agency, explicit rules, competition, spectators, or a fixed goal and ask what
remains recognizable as play. "

> bracketing -> close description of experience -> variation on experience -> disclose the structural conditions

### 8. Hermeneutic interpretation

The hermeneutic circle; movement between part and whole. The technique of close
reading.

> initial observation -> close reading -> revised whole reading -> revised close reading ...

### 9. Genealogy

Nietzsche, Foucault.

How did the concept become necessary, natural, or authoritative for us?
Its movement is roughly:

1. Begin with a present category or value that appears self-evident.
2. Recover heterogeneous practices and conflicts hidden by that apparent unity.
3. Show how the concept was produced through accidents, institutions,
   exclusions, and struggles.
4. Return to the present with the concept denaturalized.

A genealogy of play might examine how play became separated from labor, assigned
to childhood, connected to leisure, disciplined by education, monetized, or
recruited into productivity. This seems highly relevant to your “play and
conscription” material.

Genealogy is not simply chronological history. Its purpose is diagnostic: to
loosen the present concept’s claim to necessity.

### 10. Transcendental argument

What must be true, given the facts?

> We do X -> X entails Y -> therefore Y must obtain

Similar to socratic movement toward generality, but asks about underlying universal conditions, especially of reason. Obviously this is Kant.

### 11. Geometrical or axiomatic demonstration

Proof oriented work, e.g. Spinoza.

### 12. Analytic structure: problem, argument, objection, response

The standard contemporary paper often follows:

1. State a delimited problem.
2. Define terms and distinguish neighboring claims.
3. Formulate a thesis.
4. Give an explicit argument.
5. Test it with cases or counterexamples.
6. Consider objections.
7. Revise or defend the thesis.
8. State the result’s scope.

Could be quite useful for e.g. delimiting a strict concept of play, i.e. finding
necessary and sufficient conditions for an activity to count as 'play.'

### 13. Aphorism, fragment, constellation

Nietzschean style. A bunch of observations, some recurring images, and an unresolved tension. Aporia?

### 14. Thought experiment

"What is it like to be a bat?"

> case -> intuitive judgment -> variation -> principle -> complication

### 15. Protreptic, manifesto, intervention

> diagnosis -> declaration of new possibility -> revaluation -> call to action

Protreptic writing turns the reader toward philosophy or a way of life. A
manifesto **names an emerging collective subject and speaks partly in the future
tense:** it describes what must be made as if it were already arriving.

Protrepsis = exhortation to a new way of being
Paranesis = exhortation to continue a current way of being
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protrepsis_and_paraenesis

Marx and Engels, Deleuze and Guattarri

Overall structure:

1. _Aporia_: Ask what the hell play even _is,_ get us all hot and bothered, pique
   our interest. The ordinary definitions of play fail. They don't really have a
   strong set of necessary and sufficient conditions. (Can they?)
2. _Phenomenological description_: Examine the real conditions of play. What do
   we play, when, where, and why? What are the conditions of playful activity? --
   Note this is only useful for describing _agentic_ play. The _phenomenology_
   of play. Probably draw on Fink here.
3. _Genealogy_: probably skipping this one or brushing over it lightly -- analysis
   of Hans and Huizinga. My focus is more on epistemology. Though admixing this
   with Noë's genealogy of aesthetic experience and the entanglement would be
   productive here.
4. _Reconstruction_: transformative play is a kind of inquiry
   -- not a heady one, but the one at the heart of culture.
5. _Application_: Reinterpretation of trauma and conceptualization, investigation
   of psychonalysis and the unconscious
6. _Protrepsis_: What institutions, practices, worlds should the playful inquirer
   design?

Overall book structure: Deconstruction and reconstruction. Pose the problem,
dissolve the structure, re-symbolize, see the world anew. This the argumentative
structure -- and in the book it's a _rhetorical_ structure, which is an
_aesthetic_ phenomenon, one wherein the author _plays_ with concepts and
attempts to _design_ an experience to bring the audience alongside them.

Would be cool and fun to play with structure generally. To write aphorisms
alongside geometric proof alongside traditional essay.
